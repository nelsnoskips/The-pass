# All-in-one and agentic restaurant platforms: competitors, bundling dynamics and go-to-market (US, late 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-11. Unless labeled otherwise, sources are from 2025–2026. Many pricing figures come from third-party review and comparison sites, which disagree with each other. Those are flagged.

## Open (getopen.com): product, agents, pricing, funding, traction, weaknesses

### Takeaway
Open is the closest match to The Pass's agent-first positioning. It is a DoorDash-alumni-founded "AI-native platform" whose modules are website, ordering, app, gift cards, catering, loyalty, CRM, SMS, email and push, with named agents including Paid Ads. It is still small (30+ brands as of mid-2025), and its pricing and funding are not public. Its gaps relative to The Pass are reservations, host stand, waitlist and Google review replies: it is off-premises and ecommerce first.

### Cited Findings
- Open calls itself an "AI-native platform for restaurants" that replaces "10+ legacy systems". Its listed modules are Website, Ordering, Mobile App, Gift Cards, Catering, Loyalty, Referrals, Email, SMS, Push, CRM and Reporting. Its listed AI agents are Ecommerce, Marketing, Support, SEO, Paid Ads and Catering — [Open company page](https://getopen.com/company)
- Its trust center reads: "Founded in 2024 and backed by Sequoia, we power restaurant's digital experiences and AI agents nationwide" — [Open Trust Center](https://trust.getopen.com/)
- Crunchbase's Open profile sits at the URL slug "acelerate". It lists Sequoia and a Series A, with the amount obscured — [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/acelerate)
- CEO and co-founder George Jacobs previously co-founded DoorDash Drive and founded Acelerate, a virtual-brand and delivery-aggregator company, in late 2019. Acelerate raised more than $14M from Sequoia. After the marketplaces cracked down on virtual brands around 2022, Jacobs pivoted to first-party ordering, "now called Open". The article describes AI marketing and support agents serving "30+ SMB brand customers", naming Wake And Late, Loqui and dan modern chinese (June 19, 2025) — [HNGRY](https://www.hngry.tv/articles/ex-doordash-employees-double-down-on-d2c-with-ai-powered-marketing-support-agents/)
- Acelerate's $14.44M Series A was led by Sequoia (2021, older source) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/07/14/acelerate-raises-14-44m-series-a-to-turn-existing-restaurants-into-cloud-kitchens)
- Customer testimonials on Open's site: Pop's Bagels says it is "up 400% since our launch", and Eat Fantastic says "We doubled our orders in five months" — [Open company page](https://getopen.com/company)
- Open sells an enterprise or multi-location offering — [Open Enterprise](https://getopen.com/enterprise)
- Open is listed in Toast's partner directory, so it integrates with Toast POS rather than replacing it — [Toast partner directory](https://pos.toasttab.com/partners/directory/Open)
- Open's site has a product post titled "Introducing Open Infinite" — [Open blog](https://getopen.com/blog/introducing-open-infinite)

### Inferences
- Open is effectively Acelerate rebranded and pivoted. Its Sequoia backing is real, but it dates from the Acelerate era. Reported capital is about $14M, much less than Owner's.
- Open's agent list (Ecommerce, Marketing, SEO, Paid Ads, Support, Catering) overlaps heavily with The Pass's roadmap. The wedge is first-party online ordering and app for counter-service and takeout brands such as bagels, pizza and fast casual.
- Its weakness for The Pass's target segment is dine-in. I found nothing showing that Open does reservations, host stand, waitlist, deposits or review replies. Full-service independents, the segment hit hardest by closures, are underserved by Open and Owner alike.
- Open relies on POS integrations (Toast partner) instead of owning the POS.

### Gaps
- Open's pricing: getopen.com/pricing returned 404 on 2026-11-11, and I found no third-party price.
- Open's 2025–2026 funding amount and current customer count.
- G2, Capterra and Reddit reviews of Open: none found. Its weaknesses are inferred from product scope, not from customer complaints.
- Whether Open's agents need owner approval before spending money or messaging guests.
- A Food On Demand headline (Oct 6, 2026), "Off-Prem-Focused AI Agent Startup Raises $4M", could not be tied to a company, so it may or may not be Open — [Food On Demand](https://foodondemand.com/10062026/off-prem-focused-ai-agent-startup-raises-4m/)

## Incumbents and agentic competitors: Owner.com, Toast, Square, SevenRooms/DoorDash, Popmenu, AI phone, other startups

### Takeaway
Two players have money and agent narratives: Owner.com ($2.3B valuation, more than $100M ARR, flat $499/month) and Toast (189K locations, Toast IQ "Grow" agents sold as upsells). DoorDash now owns SevenRooms, which carries data-trust questions. Popmenu bundles website, marketing and AI phone answering behind demos and add-ons. Voice AI is a hot, separately funded category: Slang $68M total, Hostie (formerly Maitre-D) $16M, Loman $3.5M.

### Cited Findings
**Owner.com**
- $240M Series D at a $2.3B valuation, led by Goldman Sachs Alternatives Growth Equity with Meritech, Redpoint, Headline and Jack Altman (Aug 28, 2026). ARR has passed $100M. Owner says it powers "more U.S. locations than Domino's or Taco Bell" and that independents will drive more than $1B in sales through it in 2026. Its products are websites, online ordering, mobile apps, CRM, customer support, POS and AI phone ordering. Its agents create promotions, update the site, build campaigns and creative, answer the phone, take orders, and reply to reviews and emails. It plans to expand into salons, spas and grocers, and internationally. Reported results: +40% online traffic within 30 days, and more than 40% direct online revenue growth in year one — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/owner-raises-240m-led-by-goldman-sachs-alternatives-to-build-the-ai-native-platform-for-every-local-business-302862420.html); [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/owner-raises-240m-ai-native-restaurant-tech)
- $120M Series C at a $1B valuation in May 2025, about 5x its value a year earlier — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-05-13/restaurant-tech-startup-owner-com-hits-1-billion-valuation); [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/tech-supplier-ownercom-raises-120m-giving-it-1b-valuation)
- Pricing is widely reported as a flat $499/month with no commission on orders. One aggregator instead reports three plans at $249–$499/month plus a 5% fee per order charged to the diner. Sources conflict — [Capterra pricing](https://www.capterra.com/p/10002488/Owner/pricing/); [CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/food-delivery-platform/owner-com/); [Sauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/owner-com-pricing-fees)
- Capterra sentiment is about 89–92% positive across about 96–110 reviews. Price feedback says "not cheap", and $499 is hard to justify for low-volume single locations — [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/10002488/Owner/)
- GTM: Owner started with door-knocking and thousands of cold emails. It was fully sales-led (demo, then salesperson, then onboarding specialist) until a free AI "Grader" replaced that path. Grader checks about 90 SEO and CRO factors and builds a finished site, upscaled photos, video and an audit in about five minutes before the prospect pays — [First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/inside-owners-zigzag-path-to-a-billion-dollar-business); [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/owner-com-did-an-ai-rebuild-to-accelerate-past-100m-arr-6-lessons-and-what-it-costs-to-apply-them/)
- Owner's stated insight: "every time a customer logs in, Owner has failed." The product is built for agents to run in the background — [SaaStr](https://saastr.com/vertical-b2b-leader-owner-acclerated-after-100m-arr-with-agents-the-key-insight-every-time-a-customer-logs-in-owner-has-failed)
- In March 2024 (older source), CRO Kyle Norton cited a 4.5:1 LTV:CAC and a burn multiple under 1, and credited merging onboarding into sales — [GetLatka interview](https://getlatka.com/companies/owner.com/interviews/owner-com-kyle-norton-2024)
- A third-party estimate of Owner's sales mix is 60–70% inbound, 30–40% outbound and 5–10% partner. This is not company-reported — [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/owner)

**Toast**
- Q3 2026: about 189,000 locations (+21% YoY) after 8,500 net adds. ARR is $2.5B (+24%) and SaaS ARR grew 28%. Total take rate is 104 bps, and the payments take rate of 50 bps rose partly on "small targeted price moves". Sales and marketing spend was $171M. Toast IQ Grow (marketing agent) is "on track to reach $10 million in ARR by the end of the year" and is sold mainly by the upsell team. Grow added SEO, reviews and offers agents. Toast IQ Team launched with a scheduling agent, with inventory and bookkeeping planned. Toast Local, its consumer app, includes reservations via Toast Tables, Resy and Tock. Online ordering has about 50M active user accounts. When asked about AI lowering software barriers, management cited data from roughly 200K locations (e.g., supplier cost benchmarking) as its moat (Nov 4, 2026) — [Motley Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/11/04/toast-tost-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- Q1 2026: about 40,000 of 171,000 locations used Toast IQ weekly. Grow pilot users saw an average 8% sales lift, and about 40% of support interactions were resolved by AI — [Toast Q1 2026 prepared remarks](https://s28.q4cdn.com/141746709/files/doc_financials/2026/q1/TOST-1Q26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf); [Motley Fool Q1](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/07/toast-tost-q1-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- **FTC v. Toast (filed Oct 29, 2026, N.D. Cal. No. 3:26-cv-12705).** Plaintiffs are the FTC, 7 states (CO, FL, IL, IA, NE, OK, UT), LA County and San Francisco. Alleged practices:
  - deceptive "$0" Starter Kits
  - an early termination fee of $150 per remaining month, buried in a 25-page merchant agreement
  - full hardware value owed if processing didn't start within 120 days
  - an $85/month fee during temporary closures
  - autorenewals and add-ons billed without consent
  - online sign-up but offline cancellation through a weekday-only "churn line", 30 days' written notice, and 90 days for payroll
  - Toast Capital borrowers defaulting if they switched POS
  - a $0.99 diner fee
  
  Toast "strongly disagree[s]" and argues ROSCA doesn't apply to B2B — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/11/ftc-and-seven-states-sue-toast)
- One customer was charged a $5,318.87 termination fee after four years. The FTC says "at least tens of thousands" complained — [Quartz](https://qz.com/ftc-states-sue-toast-hidden-fees-subscriptions-102926); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/toast-ftc-lawsuit-hidden-fees-cancellation)

**SevenRooms / DoorDash**
- DoorDash announced the $1.2B cash acquisition of SevenRooms in May 2025, alongside Deliveroo — [NRN](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/doordash-expands-global-reach-with-3-9b-deliveroo-1-2b-sevenrooms-acquisitions); [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/sevenrooms-deal-doordash-enters-dining-room)
- DoorDash launched reservations in its "Going Out" tab on Sept 30, 2025, with a $10–12 booking credit for diners (secondary source) — [SeatSwiper guide](https://guides.seatswiper.com/doordash-sevenrooms-going-out-reservations)
- SevenRooms says restaurants still own their first-party data and that it does not flow to the DoorDash marketplace. Guest profiles now carry DoorDash signals such as "Local" and "Elite Orderer". This is the vendor's own claim — [SevenRooms blog](https://sevenrooms.com/blog/guest-data-sevenrooms/)
- Eater NY reports DoorDash marketing that pitches insight into guests who also order on DoorDash. A critic said the contracts were unseen, so what is shared can't be confirmed — [Eater NY](https://eaterny.substack.com/p/doordash-is-coming-for-your-reservations)

**Square**
- Square for Restaurants has a free POS tier. Square Marketing adds POS-powered segments and AI-assisted email content. Third-party sources describe it as not the deepest restaurant AI stack — [Runable roundup](https://runable.com/blogs/best-ai-for-restaurants). Low confidence: I found no official Square 2026 restaurant AI announcement.

**Popmenu**
- Reported tiers are about $179, $299 and $499/month (or $159/month billed annually). Ordering, catering, AI phone answering, MMS and ads boosts are add-ons with unpublished prices. AI marketing is reportedly about $300/month extra, and AI answering about $149–150 per 500 calls. Pricing is demo-gated. All of this is third-party and conflicting — [RestaurantTools.ai](https://restauranttools.ai/tools/popmenu); [AZ Restaurant Partners](https://azrestaurantpartners.com/tools/popmenu.html)

**AI phone answering and agentic startups**
- Slang AI: $36M Series B led by USVP (Feb 2026), for $68M total. It is reservation-focused for full-service restaurants and priced at about $399–$600 per location per month. It sends ordering links by SMS rather than taking orders — [Ventureburn](https://ventureburn.com/slang-ai-raises-36m-to-scale-hospitality-voice-ai/); [Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/slang-ai-proved-restaurants-need-voice-ai-now-it-needs-to-prove-they-need-slang/); [CloudTalk review](https://www.cloudtalk.io/blog/slang-ai-review/)
- Loman AI (Austin, founded 2024): $3.5M seed led by Next Coast in Aug 2025. It takes takeout and pizza phone orders with payment, and prices by quote — [Fast Casual](https://www.fastcasual.com/news/loman-ai-raises-35m-to-transform-restaurant-operations-via-voice-ai/)
- Hostie (formerly Maitre-D AI): $12M Series A led by Obvious Ventures (Jul 8, 2026), for $16M total. Revenue grew 10x YoY. It is an AI phone and concierge product — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/hostie-raises-12m-series-a-to-power-the-future-of-restaurant-hospitality-302820521.html); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/638607-61)
- ConverseNow (drive-thru and phone voice AI) raised $15M (older source) — [The Spoon](https://thespoon.tech/conversenow-raises-15m-for-restaurant-ai-assistant-tech)

### Inferences
- The category is splitting in two:
  - Off-premises and digital-first all-in-ones: Owner, Open, Popmenu.
  - Dine-in and reservations: SevenRooms/DoorDash, plus Resy and Tock, which surface inside Toast Local.
  
  No well-funded player combines reservations, host stand and deposits with website, ordering and marketing agents at a flat price for independents. That is The Pass's whitespace.
- Toast monetizes agents as paid upsells on top of POS. Grow at about $10M ARR is tiny against $2.5B, and its adoption runs through upsell reps. Owner prices AI into a flat fee. The Pass's "flat, month to month, approve-before-spend" model fits directly against the FTC allegations about Toast.
- Voice AI costs about $400–600 per location per month on its own (Slang). Bundling phone answering into a flat fee is a strong price anchor.

### Gaps
- Square's official 2025–2026 restaurant AI features.
- Owner churn and complaints from Reddit (not surfaced), and whether Owner's 2026 pricing changed.
- Superorder, Sauce and other 2024–2026 agentic startups: no confirmed rounds found.

## Market sizing and dynamics: independents, tech spend, tool sprawl, consolidation, POS gravity

### Takeaway
There are about 412K US independent restaurants, shrinking 2.3% a year, with full-service independents hit hardest. Operators are consolidating vendors, and point-solution buying is falling. POS remains the gravity center, but none of the sources I found give a defensible figure for tech spend per location.

### Cited Findings
- Technomic counted 412,498 independent restaurants at the end of 2025, down from 422,001 in 2024 (−2.3%, about −9,500). Full-service independents fell by a net 6,400. Chain units rose 1.4% to more than 263K. Independents are defined as outside Technomic's top 1,500 chains — [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/operations/number-independent-restaurants-declined-23-2025); [NRN](https://www.nrn.com/independent-restaurants/the-independent-restaurant-sector-shrunk-by-2-3-in-2025)
- Multi-unit operators average 11.4 tech vendors in 2026, down from 13.8 in early 2025, and 54% are actively consolidating (FSRI survey of 612 operators; methodology unverified, vendor-adjacent) — [FSRI](https://foodserviceresearchinstitute.com/blog/restaurant-technology-trends)
- Buyers of best-of-breed point solutions fell from 31% to 24% (Informa 2026, via a secondary source) — [Transact](https://www.transact-tech.com/resources/blog/restaurant-tech-stack-consolidation)
- 40% of operators use 4–5 separate systems for core functions, and 30% say connecting guest data across them is hardest (SevenRooms/DoorDash 2026 report, vendor source) — [SevenRooms](https://sevenrooms.com/blog/restaurant-technology-trends/)
- 97% of multi-unit full-service operators run the same POS across all venues, up from 86% (TouchBistro survey) — [FSR](https://www.fsrmagazine.com/feature/restaurants-reach-a-technology-turning-point-rooted-in-simplicity/)
- Restaurant-tech M&A is up 45% (vendor blog) — [Popcorn GTM](https://popcorngtm.com/blog/restaurant-tech-ma-is-up)
- Toast at 189K locations is roughly 28% of all US restaurants (my calculation: 189K ÷ 675K, the sum of 412K independents and 263K chain units) — [Toast Q3 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/11/04/toast-tost-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/); [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/operations/number-independent-restaurants-declined-23-2025)

### Inferences
- The addressable base is shrinking, and survivors are cost-sensitive. Saving money on point tools is a credible pitch.
- POS standardization means The Pass should integrate with Toast and Square instead of fighting them (Open does the same). Customer-facing layers are where consolidation is still open.

### Gaps
- Tech spend per independent location: I found no credible source. Toast Restaurant Trends and NRA State of the Industry were not retrieved.
- Tool counts specific to independents (the FSRI figure is for multi-unit operators).

## GTM: motions that work for independents, CAC/churn, switching triggers

### Takeaway
Owner's path is the best documented: founder door-knocking and cold email, then a sales-led demo motion, then a free AI tool that does the work before the prospect pays. Toast grows by upsell into its POS base. The clearest switching trigger right now is the FTC case against Toast and the termination-fee backlash.

### Cited Findings
- Owner's history: founder-led door-knocking and cold email, then fully sales-led, then a free Grader and AI site build as lead gen (see above) — [First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/inside-owners-zigzag-path-to-a-billion-dollar-business); [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/owner-com-did-an-ai-rebuild-to-accelerate-past-100m-arr-6-lessons-and-what-it-costs-to-apply-them/)
- Owner's 4.5:1 LTV:CAC dates from 2024 (older source) — [GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/owner.com/interviews/owner-com-kyle-norton-2024)
- Owner hires "Outbound Account Executives", so outbound sales is still active — [Welcome to the Jungle job post](https://app.welcometothejungle.com/jobs/EFnvHnqp/company)
- Toast sells Grow through upsell reps and targets payback of 20 months or less for new markets — [Motley Fool Q3](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/11/04/toast-tost-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- Toast's alleged switching costs are a $150/month termination fee per remaining month, Toast Capital default on switching POS, and offline cancellation — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/11/ftc-and-seven-states-sue-toast)
- Competitors are already using fee-increase messaging against Toast (vendor marketing) — [SLFT Payments](https://www.sleftpayments.com/learning-hub/toast-pos-raised-fees-options-2026)

### Inferences
- For The Pass: lead with the founder's network (founder-led sales), and build a free audit tool in the style of Owner's Grader. Examples: "your Google profile, site, reviews and reservation leak audit". A visible, approval-based agent log and no contract are differentiators while the FTC case is in the news.
- Switching triggers to target:
  - contract renewal dates
  - the FTC news cycle
  - DoorDash ownership of SevenRooms, for operators wary of sharing data with a marketplace
  - price increases
  - new openings and relaunches
  - website redesigns

### Gaps
- Public CAC and churn benchmarks for SMB restaurant SaaS: none found beyond Owner's 2024 LTV:CAC.
- Data on whether agencies or referrals work: none found.

## Incumbent vulnerabilities and historical wedge products

### Takeaway
Incumbents are most exposed on four fronts:
- Toast's legal and trust problem over fees, contracts and cancellation.
- DoorDash's ownership of SevenRooms, which raises data-ownership worries.
- Demo-gated add-on pricing (Popmenu).
- AI sold as paid upsells with modest adoption (about 23% weekly use of Toast IQ in Q1).

Historical wedges were POS for Toast, website plus commission-free ordering for Owner and Open, and reservations plus CRM for SevenRooms.

### Cited Findings
- FTC v. Toast allegations, filed Oct 29, 2026 — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/11/ftc-and-seven-states-sue-toast)
- 40K of 171K Toast locations used Toast IQ weekly in Q1 2026 — [Toast Q1 prepared remarks](https://s28.q4cdn.com/141746709/files/doc_financials/2026/q1/TOST-1Q26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)
- DoorDash pitches unified delivery and dining data, and critics question what is shared — [Eater NY](https://eaterny.substack.com/p/doordash-is-coming-for-your-reservations)
- Popmenu add-ons are unpublished and demo-gated — [RestaurantTools.ai](https://restauranttools.ai/tools/popmenu)
- Owner's wedge is website plus commission-free ordering, with traffic and direct revenue lift as proof — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/owner-raises-240m-led-by-goldman-sachs-alternatives-to-build-the-ai-native-platform-for-every-local-business-302862420.html)
- Open's wedge, after its pivot from Acelerate, is first-party ordering and apps — [HNGRY](https://www.hngry.tv/articles/ex-doordash-employees-double-down-on-d2c-with-ai-powered-marketing-support-agents/)

### Inferences
- The proven wedge for independents is website plus direct ordering, because it shows measurable revenue quickly. For full-service independents, reservations plus deposits (no-show protection) plus guest CRM is the equivalent, and Owner and Open don't serve it.
- The Pass's best wedge is probably a free audit and a website rebuilt in minutes (Owner's proven lead gen), converting into reservations with deposits for full-service venues. Phone and review agents are a bundled value anchor against $400–600/month voice tools.

### Gaps
- No primary historical data on which module drove Toast's or SevenRooms' platform attach. Those wedges are inferred from their product history.
