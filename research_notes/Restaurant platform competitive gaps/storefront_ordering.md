# Restaurant Website, Online Ordering and Catering Software (US, late 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-11. About 18 tool calls. Much of the pricing data comes from competitor blogs (Sauce/getsauce.com, Restolabs, UpMenu, ChowNow, Owner.com, Beyond Menu). These sellers have a reason to make rivals look expensive, and each item below is labelled with its source. Vendor-owned pages are preferred wherever they were found.

## Q1. Who leads, what does each do best, and what do they charge?

### Takeaway
For independents, the main split is between the **website plus direct-ordering bundles** (Owner.com, Popmenu, ChowNow, BentoBox, Menufy) and the **POS-native ordering** products (Toast, Square). Olo and Lunchbox are priced and built for chains. Owner.com has moved to a two-plan model: $249/month plus 5% per order, or $499/month flat. It also charges guests a 5% fee, so even its "flat" plan is not fee-free for diners. Catering is led by ezCater, a 15% commission marketplace that **Uber agreed to buy for $2.3B in October 2026**. Event and catering CRM is led by Tripleseat, which does not publish prices.

### Cited Findings

**Owner.com (website + ordering + app + loyalty + marketing)**
- Flexible plan is $249/month plus a 5% restaurant fee on orders. Flat Rate plan is $499/month with no additional restaurant fee. Processing is 2.9% + $0.30. Guests pay a 5% "order support fee". No setup fee is listed, and the page says "never locked into a contract". Pricing is stated as current as of Sept 23, 2026 (Owner's own blog). — [Owner.com blog](https://www.owner.com/blog/how-much-does-a-restaurant-website-cost)
- Owner recommends Flat Rate for restaurants doing $5k+/month in online sales. Reviewers put break-even at about $5k/month. — [search summary of Owner blog, G2 and reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/owner-com/pricing); [growwithba review](https://www.owner.com/blog/how-much-does-a-restaurant-website-cost)
- Both plans include an SEO-optimized website, online ordering, loyalty, a reviews engine, marketing campaigns, a photo upscaler, and hosting/SSL. Owner claims "some restaurants saw ~20% more Google traffic in first 30 days" (vendor claim, results vary). — [Owner.com blog](https://www.owner.com/blog/how-much-does-a-restaurant-website-cost)
- Capterra lists a month-to-month contract. Reviews are mixed: one reviewer reported online sales dropping by at least $6,000 in the first month after switching to Owner's backend, and another called it a "game-changer". — [Capterra Owner](https://capterra.com/p/10002488/Owner/)
- Owner.com raised a $33M Series B in Jan 2024 (older, for context). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/31/owner-33m-series-b-online-restaurants/)

**Popmenu (website, menus, marketing, AI phone answering)**
- Plans: Starter $179/month, Essentials $299/month, Premier $499/month, Enterprise (custom), and an AI phone-answering add-on (price undisclosed). — [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/popmenu)
- Online ordering is an add-on. Restolabs' example includes about $1 per order. Delivery relies on third parties. — [Restolabs (competitor)](https://www.restolabs.com/blog/popmenu-pricing)
- Contracts are multi-year; see the complaints under Q2.

**BentoBox (Fiserv) (design-led sites for full-service and hospitality groups)**
- No public prices. Tiers are Foundations, Signature and Elevated, with pricing on request. — [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/retail/bentobox-profile/); [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/bentobox/pricing)
- A competitor claims it starts at $49/month plus per-order fees. This is unverified and conflicts with the "contact us" listings. — [Sauce (competitor)](https://www.getsauce.com/post/bentobox-pricing-fees)

**ChowNow (commission-free direct ordering, app, catering)**
- Sources disagree on tiers. Launch is $229, Grow $319 and Elevate $409 per month, billed annually (SelectHub). Chowly (June 2026) gives a $229 to $449 range plus a $119 to $499 setup fee and 2.95% + $0.29 processing. One comparison site lists older Hub/Pro/Premier tiers at $119/$229/$328. — [SelectHub](https://www.selecthub.com/food-delivery-software/chownow/); [Chowly](https://chowly.com/resources/blogs/best-restaurant-online-ordering-systems/); [Capterra pricing](https://www.capterra.com/p/229841/ChowNow/pricing/)
- No commission on direct orders. Flex Delivery adds a per-order fee. Contract length and renewal terms are not fully spelled out. — [Chowly](https://chowly.com/alternatives/chownow-alternatives/)
- ChowNow catering is a flat monthly fee plus a **flat 5% commission on catering orders**. ChowNow's example: about $500/month on $10k of catering volume. It runs through the restaurant's own ordering site and shares data with loyalty and the app. — [ChowNow blog, May 7, 2026](https://get.chownow.com/blog/best-catering-software-for-restaurants/)

**Toast Online Ordering (POS-native)**
- Sold as an add-on to the POS. Third-party estimates run from about $75/month (basic) to about $149 (bundle). Online card processing is about 3.5% + 15¢, per competitors; Fleksa cites 2.49% + 15¢. The numbers conflict and come from competitor sources. — [UpMenu](https://www.upmenu.com/blog/toast-pricing); [Sauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/toast-online-ordering-pricing-fees); [Fleksa](https://fleksa.com/en/cost/online-ordering-fees)
- In July 2023 Toast rolled out a $0.99 guest fee on online orders of $10 or more. It reversed within days after an operator revolt and a congressional inquiry ("We made the wrong decision"). (2023, older but a key precedent.) — [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/toast-remove-99-cent-fee-after-widespread-backlash); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/restaurant-technology/2023/toast-reverses-course-on-0-99-consumer-fee-following-restaurant-outcry/); [Digital Transactions (2023)](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/toast-tests-a-new-online-customer-fee-but-will-it-stick/)
- Toast cited a 13% average increase in guests ordering via Toast Online Ordering at test restaurants. The fee was meant to fund TakeOutApp and Order with Google integrations. An analyst noted that switching is hard because Toast's software is tied to its payments. — [Digital Transactions (2023)](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/toast-tests-a-new-online-customer-fee-but-will-it-stick/)

**Square Online**
- A free plan exists, with processing charged per transaction. Third-party guides cite paid plans at about 2.9% + 30¢ online, and Premium at $149/month per location. Not verified on Square's US page. — [Sauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/square-online-ordering-pricing-fees); [Square pricing](https://squareup.com/us/en/pricing)

**Menufy / Slice**
- Menufy: $149/month on an annual plan or $179/month month to month, no commission, per a 2026 comparison. Menufy's own (2023) note describes a per-order guest convenience fee raised from $1.50 to $1.75, so it may now have a mixed model. — [hackceleration 2026](https://hackceleration.com/labs/best/restaurant-online-ordering-system); [Menufy (2023)](https://restaurant.menufy.com/article/2018/pricing-adjustment)
- Slice (pizza): reported at a fixed $2.25 per order, but the source is undated and the model is unclear. — [TrustRadius compare](https://www.trustradius.com/compare-products/slice-vs-square-for-restaurants)

**Olo / Lunchbox (chain-focused)**
- Olo uses enterprise quotes. A competitor estimates $1,500 to $3,000+/month for smaller chains. Lunchbox uses custom quotes, at about $300+/month with setup from $5,000 (competitor estimates), and targets chains of 5 to 100+ locations. — [Fleksa](https://fleksa.com/en/vs/olo); [Sauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/lunchbox-vs-restolabs)

**Catering**
- **ezCater:** no signup or monthly fees. Commission (15% in ezCater's 2025 training and sample breakdown) applies to the meal subtotal and delivery fees, not to tips or taxes. Payment fee is 2.99%. Payouts are weekly. — [ezCater help](https://catering.ezcater.com/en/help/what-is-the-commission/-payment-breakdown); [ezCater selling fees](https://www.ezcater.com/company/grow-catering-business/selling-fees/)
- **Uber is acquiring ezCater** for $2.3B cash (announced Oct 6, 2026; pending approval). ezCater has 140k+ restaurants, $2.5B+ annual gross bookings and average orders above $400. Operators are uneasy: Cowboy Chicken's off-premises director cited "Uber just raised commissions to 28%", and California Tortilla's VP of operations asked "What gets better for the operator?" — [FastCasual](https://www.fastcasual.com/articles/ubers-ezcater-deal-puts-catering-growth-margins-and-customer-loyalty-in-focus/)
- ChowNow's estimate of ezCater fees on a $360 order: about $54 commission plus about $11 processing, roughly $65. DoorDash catering commission tiers are 15/25/30%, about $90 at 25% on a $360 order. — [ChowNow blog 2026](https://get.chownow.com/blog/best-catering-software-for-restaurants/)
- **Tripleseat** (events, private dining, catering CRM): no public prices ("Request Pricing" only). One reviewer says it is pricey for small businesses. Sources conflict on whether it integrates with Toast. — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/118047/Tripleseat/); [Pulse Signal](https://getpulsesignal.com/pricing/tripleseat)
- **HoneyCart:** flat-fee, commission-free catering ordering with order forms, lead times and deposits; catering-only. — [ChowNow blog](https://get.chownow.com/blog/best-catering-software-for-restaurants/); [HoneyCart](https://gethoneycart.com/ezcater-review-and-a-commission-free-alternative/)

### Inferences
- The leading small-operator bundles run about $179 to $499/month per location, often plus per-order fees, setup fees and guest fees. A truly flat price with **no guest fee** and **no per-order fee** would be easy to see against Owner (5% guest fee), Popmenu (per-order ordering fee), Menufy (guest convenience fee) and ChowNow catering (5%).
- Uber/ezCater raises fear of higher catering commissions in 2027. That gives direct, commission-free catering ordering a timely pitch.
- Toast's 2023 reversal shows that operators react strongly to fees charged to their guests without consent. "No hidden guest fees" is a credible message.

### Gaps
- Thanx ordering pricing, Toast catering & events add-on pricing, and DoorDash Drive and Uber Direct official per-delivery rates were not found. Older Square-hosted figures cite DoorDash at $6.99 for up to 5 miles plus $1 per extra mile, and Uber at $4.20 + $0.75/km (undated). — [Square ODD pricing (undated)](https://squareup.com/us/en/legal/general/odd-pricing)
- Square US online-ordering plan pricing and Slice's current model were not confirmed from primary sources.
- Toast and Square earnings commentary on online ordering in 2026 was not researched, due to the tool budget.

## Q2. What do operators complain about?

### Takeaway
The best-documented complaints are **contract lock-in and billing after cancellation** (Popmenu), **fees stacked on top of "commission-free"** (per-order, guest and processing fees), **fees imposed without consent** (Toast in 2023), **marketplace commissions** (15 to 30% delivery, ezCater 15%, possibly rising under Uber), **menu and 86 sync** across tablets and POS, and **lost sales after migrating** (one Owner review). Reddit threads were not surfaced by search, so operator voices here come from Trustpilot, BBB, Capterra and trade press.

### Cited Findings
- Popmenu: reviewers say they kept being charged after cancelling in writing, on Zoom and by email. Popmenu's BBB replies say the customer "does not have an early termination right absent Popmenu's breach". One reviewer says a buyout required paying out the rest of a **24-month** contract. Another was told "18 months and then month to month" but the signed terms differed. Setup costs were not clearly disclosed. There were 7 BBB complaints in 3 years. — [BBB Popmenu](https://www.bbb.org/us/ga/atlanta/profile/marketing-software/popmenu-0443-28164736/complaints); [Trustpilot Popmenu](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/popmenu.com)
- A former Popmenu CX manager on Glassdoor said the company is "unwilling to rightfully dissolve contracts when expectations are not met" (a single employee review). — [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Reviews/Employee-Review-Popmenu-E2224332-RVW80064213.htm)
- Owner.com: one Capterra reviewer reports online sales dropped by at least $6k in the first month after switching. — [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/10002488/Owner/)
- Toast's 2023 guest fee was "made remotely by Toast, without [the owner's] consent" and set off a "mom-and-pop restaurant revolt". — [Fox Business](https://www-ak-ms.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/mom-pop-restaurant-revolt-forces-big-tech-vendor-toast-rescind-fees-american-diners); [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/toast-remove-99-cent-fee-after-widespread-backlash)
- NRN covered "fee fatigue" across restaurant tech after the Toast episode. — [NRN](https://nrn.com/restaurant-technology/here-s-how-fee-fatigue-is-affecting-the-restaurant-technology-industry)
- "Commission-free" covers only one line item: processing, subscriptions, delivery and diner fees still apply (from a vendor blog). — [Beyond Menu](https://get.beyondmenu.com/blog/commission-free-online-ordering-restaurants)
- DoorDash marketplace charges 15/25/30% on delivery and 6% on pickup, and pickup requires in-store price matching. — [Paymentreview 2026](https://paymentreview.com/blog/restaurant-online-ordering-fees/); [Orderitto](https://orderitto.com/blog/how-much-does-doordash-charge-restaurants)
- Typical operational pain: a separate tablet for each delivery app, staff retyping orders into the POS, menus drifting out of sync, and **86'd items still selling online**. — [Smart Restaurant Owner](https://smartrestaurantowner.com/blog/restaurant-online-ordering-guide); [Richmenu](https://richmenu.io/restaurant-online-ordering/)
- Marketplace catering: the platform owns the buyer, repeat orders keep paying commission, and the restaurant gets little buyer data. Commissions do not fall as order size grows. — [ChowNow blog 2026](https://get.chownow.com/blog/best-catering-software-for-restaurants/)
- Catering service risk: late deliveries disrupt meetings and put recurring accounts at risk. Catering needs vehicle capacity, temperature control and building access. — [FastCasual](https://www.fastcasual.com/articles/ubers-ezcater-deal-puts-catering-growth-margins-and-customer-loyalty-in-focus/)

### Inferences
- The top trust levers are a month-to-month contract, one-click cancellation, no guest fees, and all-in pricing shown up front.
- The Pass's agentic site edits with owner approval directly answer the "slow site edits / waiting on support" pain. That pain is plausible but was **not** directly sourced here (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No Reddit threads (r/restaurateur, r/restaurantowners, r/KitchenConfidential) were retrieved. Search did not surface them, so Reddit sentiment is unverified.
- Complaints about BentoBox site-edit turnaround, template sameness and SEO claims were not found in sources.
- Capterra and G2 review pages for Owner returned 403, so aggregate ratings are missing.

## Q3. Table stakes vs differentiators

### Takeaway
Based on the products surveyed, **table stakes** for direct ordering are item modifiers, real-time 86/pause synced to the POS, scheduled orders, Apple Pay and Google Pay, mobile-first menus, Google ordering links, and some form of delivery (usually DoorDash Drive or Uber Direct white-label, as with ChowNow Flex Delivery). **Differentiators** are loyalty and CRM tied to ordering, a branded app, automated marketing, AI phone answering (Popmenu), and catering that runs in the same system with deposits, lead times and invoices.

### Cited Findings
- Owner bundles website, ordering, app, loyalty, reviews engine and marketing in every plan. — [Owner.com blog](https://www.owner.com/blog/how-much-does-a-restaurant-website-cost)
- Popmenu sells an AI phone-answering add-on. — [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/popmenu)
- ChowNow offers Flex Delivery (white-label delivery for a per-order fee) and catering on the same branded site with shared loyalty data. — [Chowly](https://chowly.com/alternatives/chownow-alternatives/); [ChowNow blog](https://get.chownow.com/blog/best-catering-software-for-restaurants/)
- Toast says it invested in **Order with Google** integration and TakeOutApp, so ordering from Google surfaces is expected (2023). — [Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/toast-tests-a-new-online-customer-fee-but-will-it-stick/)
- Catering-specific features operators look for: order forms, lead times, deposits (HoneyCart), and order and delivery minimums. — [ChowNow blog](https://get.chownow.com/blog/best-catering-software-for-restaurants/)
- 86'd items should disappear from the online menu immediately. — [Richmenu](https://richmenu.io/restaurant-online-ordering/)

### Inferences
- Modifiers and POS hand-off are the two biggest missing table stakes in The Pass. Almost every ordering product has modifiers, and operators on Toast or Square will not re-key orders.
- Delivery is expected but can be white-label via DoorDash Drive or Uber Direct, without The Pass running couriers.

### Gaps
- No source quantified how much Google's "Order online" button contributes to conversion, and no source confirmed the current Order with Google partner requirements.
- Upsell and AOV-lift data was not found.

## Q4. Gaps and quick wins for The Pass

### Takeaway
The Pass's flat per-location price with no commission is a real wedge. It holds only if there are **no guest fees** and **no lock-in**, and if the missing table stakes are closed: modifiers, POS hand-off, delivery via Drive/Direct, Google ordering, and catering quotes/deposits/invoices. Catering is the timeliest opening because Uber/ezCater raises commission fears and ezCater's average order is over $400.

### Cited Findings
- Owner charges guests 5% and $249 + 5% per order on its entry plan. ChowNow takes 5% on catering. Popmenu charges per order for ordering. — [Owner](https://www.owner.com/blog/how-much-does-a-restaurant-website-cost); [ChowNow](https://get.chownow.com/blog/best-catering-software-for-restaurants/); [Restolabs](https://www.restolabs.com/blog/popmenu-pricing)
- ezCater takes about 15% + 2.99% on orders averaging over $400, and Uber's ownership is pending. — [ezCater](https://catering.ezcater.com/en/help/what-is-the-commission/-payment-breakdown); [FastCasual](https://www.fastcasual.com/articles/ubers-ezcater-deal-puts-catering-growth-margins-and-customer-loyalty-in-focus/)
- Operators say "commission-free" products still pile on fees. — [Beyond Menu](https://get.beyondmenu.com/blog/commission-free-online-ordering-restaurants)

### Inferences (recommendations, not sourced facts)
1. **Item modifiers and options** with required/optional groups and min/max rules, priced server-side. This is the highest-priority missing table stake.
2. **Catering workflow**: quote, then deposit via Stripe, then invoice with balance due, plus lead-time and minimum rules, headcount-based packages and reorder links. Market it as "keep your ezCater regulars direct: 0% commission". Easy wins: a "reorder last catering order" SMS/email to CRM contacts, and importing ezCater customer lists the operator already has.
3. **Delivery via DoorDash Drive / Uber Direct API** (white-label), with the fee passed to the guest at cost. This covers delivery without marketplace commission.
4. **Google "Order online" / Reserve with Google** links pointing at The Pass storefront.
5. **POS hand-off** (Toast and Square first) so operators don't re-key orders or keep a second 86 list. Until that exists, a fast 86 from the kitchen board and an SMS command to the agent ("86 the halibut") is a quick win.
6. **Trust pricing page**: an all-in calculator compared with Owner, Popmenu, ChowNow and ezCater at the operator's own volume, month-to-month terms, self-serve cancellation, and "we never add fees to your guests". This targets the Popmenu lock-in and Toast/Owner guest-fee complaints.
7. **Agent edits as the differentiator**: same-day plain-language site and menu changes with an approval log, set against the slow-edit and support-ticket model (complaint not directly sourced; validate with interviews).
8. **Migration guarantee and conversion monitoring**: one Owner reviewer lost $6k in the first month after switching, so offer redirect and SEO preservation plus an agent that watches order volume after the switch.
9. Apple Pay and Google Pay through Stripe Payment Element, scheduled orders, and order throttling per time slot (kitchen capacity) are low-effort additions if not already present.

### Gaps
- No operator-survey data on willingness to switch, or on how much flat pricing matters versus features.
- Feasibility and pricing of DoorDash Drive and Uber Direct for small merchants need confirmation from official pages.
