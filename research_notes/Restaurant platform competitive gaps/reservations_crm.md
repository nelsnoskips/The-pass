## Leaders, positioning, ownership and current pricing

### Takeaway
The market has become three bundles, each owned by a company with another business to protect: OpenTable (Booking Holdings, ~60,000 restaurants, now pushing to be the "system of record"), Amex's Resy+Tock (~25,000 venues, a card-perk funnel), and DoorDash's SevenRooms (folded into "DashOS" with delivery, loyalty and AI voice). POS-native add-ons (Toast Tables, Yelp Guest Manager) sit in the $129–$199/month band. No independent, flat-priced, restaurant-owned option has momentum in 2026.

### Inferences
- Each big player now subsidizes diners with someone else's money: Amex card credits, DoorDash's $10–12 credits, OpenTable points. That builds diner loyalty to the network rather than the restaurant, and The Pass's restaurant-owned loyalty is the counter-position.
- DoorDash's "no cover fees ever" on in-app bookings removes the per-cover-fee argument as a differentiator against SevenRooms specifically. The Pass must differentiate there on data ownership and neutrality, not price.
- DashOS (one system, AI voice, loyalty, marketing) is the closest analogue to The Pass's all-in-one vision. Its weakness for independents is that it is tied to the delivery marketplace, has no public pricing, and its loyalty arrives in 2027.

## Operator complaints 2025–2026 (new evidence beyond FRICTION.md)

### Takeaway
The biggest new grievance is contractual. OpenTable's March 2026 terms (12-month auto-renew, heavy early-termination liability, unilateral fee changes after the initial term, "primary system" plus inventory requirements, a 2% cut of deposits and no-show fees) drew a state AG review request. Data-continuity failures during switches are a second concrete pain. Measured no-show rates are far lower than folklore.

### Cited Findings
- OpenTable's March 2026 agreement: cancelling for convenience mid-term costs 100% of remaining subscription fees plus 100% of remaining cover fees in the initial term (50% of remaining subscription plus 100% of cover fees in a renewal term). After the initial term OpenTable may "in its sole discretion modify the fees listed on the Order" — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/why-restaurants-anxious-opentable-terms-105500851.html); [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/opentables-grip-tightens-restaurants-rebel-against-primary-system-mandate/) *(snippets)*
- OpenTable requires partners to make it their "system of record" for reservations, table and guest management, and to make all their inventory available on the OpenTable marketplace. The Wolfgang Puck group, which uses several platforms, was weighing its response — [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/opentable-will-require-restaurants-make-it-their-primary-reservations-system) *(snippet)*
- OpenTable's justification: the terms protect guest data from scrapers and from bad actors replicating bookings elsewhere, and annual terms bring it "in line with industry standards" — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/why-restaurants-anxious-opentable-terms-105500851.html) *(snippet)*
- Marcos Wanless (Seattle Latino Metropolitan Chamber of Commerce) asked the Washington AG to review the terms. The antitrust division said it would review whether they "may constitute an anticompetitive practice" — [Washington State Standard, Apr 15 2026](https://washingtonstatestandard.com/2026/04/15/opentables-new-rules-have-a-seattle-business-leader-calling-foul/) *(snippet)*
- Restaurants running a rival system alongside OpenTable (e.g. SevenRooms integration) reportedly faced about $250/month per location for dual use. This figure comes from older reporting — [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/opentables-grip-tightens-restaurants-rebel-against-primary-system-mandate/) *(snippet; unverified)*
- Data loss on switching: after Union Square Cafe moved from Resy to SevenRooms, "the restaurant's notes on some guests weren't showing up in the system." USHG then moved from DoorDash-owned SevenRooms to OpenTable (June 2026). The reasons are paywalled — [Caper, Jun 4 2026](https://caper.media/p/union-square-hospitality-group-reservation-move-sevenrooms-opentable)
- **No-show numbers (measured, not surveyed):** resOS No-Show Index (Aug 2025–Jul 2026, 3.77M reservations, 2,417 restaurants): 2.33% average, 2.63% at dinner and 1.84% at lunch; 0.80% with card or deposit protection vs 2.47% without (association, not causation) — [resOS](https://resos.com/restaurant-no-show-rate/). Bloom Intelligence (641k reservations since Jan 2025): 3.7% no-shows plus 15% cancellations — [Bloom Intelligence](https://bloomintelligence.com/blog/restaurant-no-shows/). OpenTable claims deposits cut no-shows 60% globally and says a no-show table for two costs about $80 on average — [ainora.lt roundup](https://ainora.lt/blog/restaurant-no-show-statistics-2026) *(snippet, secondary)*. resOS is non-US-weighted (likely Europe-heavy).
- **Resale laws (new since FRICTION.md):** California AB 1640 (the Restaurant Reservation Anti-Piracy Act, successor to AB 1245) passed the Assembly in late May 2026 and would bar reselling a reservation above its original price. Its latest tracked status was the Senate Judiciary Committee, and the current status is unverified — [CalRest](https://www.calrest.org/news/california-state-assembly-passes-bill-crack-down-black-market-restaurant-reservations); [LegiScan](https://legiscan.com/CA/bill/AB1640/2172). Florida, Hawaii and Nevada have passed similar laws; Philadelphia's ordinance allows fines up to $1,000 per unauthorized resold reservation; New Jersey is considering one — [SC Daily Gazette, Jun 2025](https://scdailygazette.com/2025/06/27/black-market-dining-reservations-sell-for-thousands-states-want-to-stop-that/); [Audacy/KYW](https://www.audacy.com/kywnewsradio/news/local/philadelphia-city-council-bill-restaurant-reservation-apps) *(snippets)*. NY's law requires a written agreement before third parties list reservations, with a $1,000/day fine per restaurant — [Holland & Knight, Feb 2025](https://hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/02/new-york-curbs-scalping-of-restaurant-reservations)

### Inferences
- At measured 2–4% no-show rates, blanket deposits mostly add friction. The Pass's case for deposits should target the slots that get resold or no-shown (prime Fri/Sat, large parties), which matches the existing FRICTION plan. Selling "deposits on everything" would overstate the problem.
- The OpenTable contract is a live switching trigger with a calendar. Restaurants that signed in spring 2026 face renewals in spring 2027, and the renewal-term termination penalty is lower (50% of subscription), so outreach timed about 60–90 days before anniversary dates is plausible.
- Spreading resale laws make "platform-authorized booking only" and verifiable phone-bound bookings a compliance feature The Pass can market, especially in CA if AB 1640 passes.

### Gaps
- No Reddit, G2 or Capterra review text could be read directly, so current sentiment on DoorDash-owned SevenRooms (e.g. "my guest data now feeds DoorDash") is **not evidenced** beyond the exec's "TBD" remark.
- No hard number on how many restaurants left OpenTable over the 2026 terms.
- AB 1640's final 2026 outcome is unverified.

## Switching costs and switching triggers

### Takeaway
Switching is hard because of discovery-network traffic, staff retraining, contract penalties (now explicit at OpenTable) and imperfect migration of guest notes and history. The triggers in 2025–2026 are concrete: acquisitions (SevenRooms→DoorDash, Tock→Resy with app sunset), OpenTable's new terms and its 2% payments fee, and per-cover bills.

### Cited Findings
- Contract exit cost at OpenTable: the remaining subscription plus cover fees for the term — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/why-restaurants-anxious-opentable-terms-105500851.html) *(snippet)*
- A forced migration: Tock venues move into Resy, and the Tock app and site are sunset — [The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/news/amex-resy-credit-tock-restaurants/?utm_section=ts) *(snippet)*. That is an involuntary re-platforming for every Tock customer, a natural moment to re-evaluate.
- Guest-note and history loss in a migration (Resy→SevenRooms at Union Square Cafe) — [Caper](https://caper.media/p/union-square-hospitality-group-reservation-move-sevenrooms-opentable)
- Large groups switch both ways (USHG back to OpenTable). Discovery and network reach still pull operators back — [Caper](https://caper.media/p/union-square-hospitality-group-reservation-move-sevenrooms-opentable)
- Diner-side subsidies (DoorDash $10–12 credits, Amex Resy credits) make network channels stickier for diners — [Food On Demand](https://foodondemand.com/05122026/doordash-ramps-up-reservations-with-largest-expansion-yet/); [AwardWallet](https://awardwallet.com/news/amex-membership-rewards/resy-credit-tock-venues/)

### Inferences
- The Pass's import (dry run, guests plus future bookings) should explicitly cover **guest notes, tags, allergies and visit history**. That is where switching visibly fails, and showing a hosted "nothing lost" check could be the selling moment.
- OpenTable's "primary system" requirement weakens The Pass's "run alongside your old system" plan. A restaurant can't make The Pass its system of record without breaching OpenTable's terms. The Pass needs a clear mode where it sits beside OpenTable and only takes direct (website, Google, Instagram) bookings, or else it must be the primary system outright.

### Gaps
- No public data on what share of covers independents get from network discovery vs direct. This decides how big the "discovery loss" fear really is.

## Gaps and wedge opportunities for The Pass

### Takeaway
The open position is neutral, flat-priced, restaurant-owned software with no marketplace or delivery interest, sold month to month. Every major alternative now carries a parent's agenda: Booking (OpenTable), Amex (Resy/Tock) or DoorDash (SevenRooms/DashOS). The Pass already has most of the product. The gaps are migration fidelity, a coexistence mode, AI phone answering (DoorDash now has it) and timing outreach to switching triggers.

### Cited Findings
- Competitors bundle AI voice answering (SevenRooms Voice AI), marketplace-insight marketing and omnichannel loyalty — [DoorDash IR](https://ir.doordash.com/news/news-details/2026/DoorDash-Introduces-DashOS-One-Platform-to-Help-Restaurants-Grow/default.aspx)
- OpenTable charges 2% on deposits, no-show fees and prepaid; Resy/Tock 2–3% on prepayments (FRICTION.md); OpenTable's 2% since Jan 2026 — [TableHelm](https://www.tablehelm.com/blog/opentable-2-percent-service-fee-explained) *(snippet)*
- Tool sprawl: 40% of operators run 4–5 systems (vendor survey) — [DoorDash IR](https://ir.doordash.com/news/news-details/2026/DoorDash-Introduces-DashOS-One-Platform-to-Help-Restaurants-Grow/default.aspx)
- Reported price points for the POS-native competitors: Toast Tables Plus about $199/month and Yelp Guest Manager about $129/month — [frontdeskreview](https://frontdeskreview.com/software/restaurant-reservation/toast-tables.md); [FitGap](https://us.fitgap.com/search/reservation-software/restaurants) *(snippets)*

### Inferences (wedges, ranked)
1. **"Neutral" positioning.** "Your guests are never used to sell delivery, card perks or other restaurants." It is a direct counter to DashOS's use of marketplace data and to OpenTable's system-of-record clause. Back it with a contract clause (no exclusivity, no inventory requirements, month to month, export any time).
2. **Switch kits per trigger.** Dedicated importers and landing pages for (a) Tock venues facing the forced Resy migration, (b) OpenTable customers approaching renewal under the 2026 terms, and (c) SevenRooms customers uneasy about DoorDash. The import must keep notes, tags and visit history, which is the known failure in the USHG/Union Square Cafe case.
3. **Price anchor.** The flat price per location should land at or below the POS-native band ($129–$199) to beat Yelp and Toast on cost. The pitch: $0 platform cut on deposits vs OpenTable's 2%, and no cover fees on any channel.
4. **Deposits done right.** With measured no-shows at 2–4%, offer targeted deposits (prime slots, big parties, flagged phones) instead of blanket ones, and market a resale-law-compliant booking (phone-bound, non-transferable) in NY, FL, HI, NV, Philadelphia and (pending) CA.
5. **Parity gaps to close.** AI phone answering for reservations (competitors now ship it), host-stand offline mode (already on the roadmap), and Reserve with Google as the stand-in for a discovery network.
6. **Coexistence mode.** For OpenTable restaurants, The Pass takes direct bookings only and isn't the system of record, until the contract ends. This needs legal review of OpenTable's terms.

### Gaps
- No verified operator willingness-to-pay data for a flat all-in-one at independent scale.
- The coexistence mode's legal compatibility with OpenTable's 2026 agreement is unverified; the primary text was not read.
- Wisely (Olo), Eat App paid tiers, TableIn and Tablz positioning and pricing were not established.
