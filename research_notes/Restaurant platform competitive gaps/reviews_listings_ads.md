# Reviews/Reputation, Listings/Local SEO, and Paid Ads for US Restaurants (late 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-11. Method note: Reddit (r/restaurateur, r/restaurantowners, r/PPC, r/FacebookAds) could not be searched directly. The search tool blocks reddit.com, and general searches returned no Reddit threads. Operator sentiment below therefore comes from BBB/Trustpilot/G2, forums (LocalSearchForum), trade press and vendor/agency blogs, and the vendor and agency sources are labeled. Many pricing figures come from third-party aggregators (CostBench, SocialPilot, Toolradar) that are often affiliated with competitors, so they are flagged as such.

## Reviews/reputation: who leads, what they do and charge, and whether AI auto-replies help or hurt

### Takeaway
Reputation tools fall into three price bands. Enterprise suites (Birdeye, Podium) run about $299–$599+/location/month on annual auto-renewing contracts. Restaurant specialists (Marqii, Ovation) run about $90–$250/location/month. POS-bundled tools (Toast Reviews Agent in Toast IQ Grow) handle Google only. Every serious player now drafts replies with AI, and the leading pattern is human approval before posting (Toast). Google itself is testing native AI replies inside GBP, which turns basic drafting into a free feature. Evidence that canned or templated replies hurt is mostly indirect: Google silently removes many templated replies to 5-star reviews, and consumers are put off by generic responses.

SocialPilot lists Growth at $349 instead — [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=721229). Businesses with 4+ locations get custom quotes — [TrueReview (competitor)](https://www.truereview.co/post/birdeye-pricing-explained-and-cheaper-alternatives).

**Google's AI replies, rejected replies, and policy**
- Google is testing AI-generated review replies inside Google Business Profile (US, Brazil and India; Europe largely excluded). Owners review and edit before publishing, and some tests allowed bulk replies — [SE Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-reply-to-reviews-with-al-41095.html); [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/google-tests-ai-review-responses-97-read-reviews); [SEO Sherpa](https://seosherpa.com/google-tests-ai-replies-to-reviews/).
- Critics say generic AI replies "could be risky and erode trust," especially on negative reviews. DesignRush cites "nearly half" of consumers as discouraged by generic replies (secondary statistic) — [Search Engine Land guide](https://searchengineland.com/guide/bad-owner-replies-google-reviews-impact); [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/google-tests-ai-review-responses-97-read-reviews).
- GMBapi studied 12,752 rejected GBP replies (June 2026). 92.6% answered 5-star reviews (templated, enthusiastic language). Rejected replies averaged about 50 days after the review (bulk or late scheduling). Rejections jumped from 398 in 2023 to 9,393 in 2024. Google deletes replies without notifying the business — [SE Roundtable, June 10, 2026](https://www.seroundtable.com/rejected-google-review-replies-analysis-41457.html). A Sterling Sky study reportedly found owners believe their replies are live when customers never see them — [Mean Business podcast](https://www.buzzsprout.com/1832299/episodes/19360305-google-is-silently-rejecting-your-review-replies-what-to-do-now).
- FTC Consumer Review Rule (16 CFR 465, effective Oct 2024): it bans fake reviews (AI-generated reviews count as fake), sentiment-conditioned incentives and review suppression. The FTC sent warning letters in late 2025/Jan 2026, including over "review gating" tools — [Morgan Lewis 2024](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/08/ftc-issues-final-rule-on-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials); [Arnold & Porter, Jan 2026](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/consumer-products-and-retail-navigator/2026/01/ftc-warning-letters-over-consumer-review-rule); [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/five-stars-zero-tolerance-ftc-turns-up-enforcement-under-consumer-review-rule/). Toast states that guests at every star rating see the Google review option, "in line with Google's policies" — [Toast FAQ](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Toast-Reviews-Agent-FAQ).

### Inferences
- AI drafting of review replies is now table stakes: Google offers it free in GBP, Toast bundles it, and Marqii sells it at $180/location. The Pass's Reviews Agent cannot win on drafting alone. It should win on context only The Pass has: the guest's reservation, party size, visit history, server/shift, and loyalty status. For example, "Sorry the wait was long Saturday; your 7:30 table seated at 7:55" can be checked against booking data. It should also win on multi-platform coverage (Toast is Google only).
- The GMBapi data suggests replies should be timely (within days, not weeks) and varied, and that thanks on 5-star reviews should be short and specific. Approval queues that sit for weeks are counterproductive. The Pass could auto-expire stale drafts, or let owners pre-approve a policy such as "auto-post 4–5 star replies under N words after review by the policy check."
- Given the FTC rule, review solicitation must go to all guests (no gating by star rating), and the change log should prove that. That compliance record is a selling point.
- Contract lock-in (Podium, Birdeye annual auto-renewals) is a major pain point. The Pass's flat monthly, presumably cancellable pricing is a differentiator if it stays month-to-month.

### Gaps
- Official pricing for Reputation.com and Yelp's review tools could not be retrieved. Yext's review product pricing was not found separately from listings.
- No restaurant-specific, quantified guest backlash to canned replies was found beyond the "nearly half" secondary statistic.
- No Google policy page explicitly allows or forbids AI-drafted owner replies was retrieved. Searchengineland.com returned 403.
- Square review-response features were not found.

## Listings/local SEO: Marqii, Yext, Moz Local, Owner.com SEO, holiday hours, menu sync, Reserve/Order buttons, and hijacking

### Takeaway
Listings sync is commoditized at about $16–$90/location/month (Moz Local at the low end, Marqii Base at $90 with POS menu sync, Yext at $199–$999/location/year). Marqii leads on restaurant menu sync from POS. The real operator pain is not pushing data but Google overriding it: suggested edits from users and third parties, auto-changes sourced from delivery platforms, robocall "verification" that changes hours, and hijacking. The "Reserve a table" button on Google requires a certified Reserve with Google partner integration.

### Cited Findings
- Marqii Base ($90/location/month, annual) pushes hours, menus and FAQ to 70+ publishers, with automated menu updates from POS integrations (Toast, Olo, ChowNow, MarketMan) — [Marqii pricing](https://www.marqii.com/pricing); [Marqii x Toast integration](https://intercom.help/toast-central/en/articles/14726292-get-started-with-the-marqii-integration). Toast's help says changes reflect "within minutes," while a Marqii testimonial says "the next day" — [Toast Central](https://intercom.help/toast-central/en/articles/14726292-get-started-with-the-marqii-integration); [Marqii menu management](https://marqii.com/menu-management). Marqii also markets "SEO and AEO" (AI answer engine optimization) — [Marqii](https://marqii.com/improve-seo-and-aeo).
- Yext self-serve tiers are reported at $199 (Emerging), $449 (Essential), $499 (Complete) and $999 (Premium) per location per year (third-party, Aug 2026), with 200+ publishers worldwide — [SocialPilot Yext alternatives](https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=712068); [WiserNotify](https://wisernotify.com/blog/moz-local-vs-yext/).
- Moz Local costs about $16–$40/location/month, each address needs its own subscription, and it covers about 90+ directories in the US, UK and Canada only — [CostBench Moz Local](https://costbench.com/software/seo-tools/moz-local/); [Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/best-local-seo-tools); [WiserNotify](https://wisernotify.com/blog/moz-local-vs-yext/).
- Owner.com claims a "20% growth in Google traffic within 30 days" of site launch (promotional, sponsored source) — [MySubscriptionAddiction (sponsored)](https://www.mysubscriptionaddiction.com/5-reasons-owner-is-the-best-tool-for-restaurant-owners-and-why-the-hype-is-real).
- **Wrong hours / suggested edits:** Anyone can suggest edits, and edits can go live if notification emails are missed. The suggested-edit system cannot be turned off. A vendor cites a restaurant that lost lunch sales when Google auto-updated hours based on a delivery platform — [Synup](https://synup.com/how-to/prevent-suggested-edits-unauthorized-changes-on-your-google-business-profile/); [Search Engine Land: when Google changes your GBP info](https://searchengineland.com/google-changes-gbp-information-439655); [Search Engine Journal: GBP info sources](https://searchenginejournal.com/google-business-profile-info-sources/446399).
- **Robocall hours verification:** On LocalSearchForum, practitioners report robocalls ("Google Maps") asking about hours. A staffer answered 8:30 instead of 8:00 and the listing was updated the next day. Others said Google changed hours arbitrarily after they stopped answering — [LocalSearchForum thread](https://localsearchforum.com/threads/im-getting-calls-from-google-maps-at-650-206-5555-asking-about-clients-hours-are-they-legit.57546/).
- **Hijacking:** "Hacking a business phone number on a Google listing is (still) easy" — [Near Media](https://nearmedia.co/p/2c516c6d-138d-4f52-b385-5b8996007088). A late-2025 wave of GBP hijackings is referenced, but the original research was not verified — [Bizcommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/someone-is-trying-to-claim-your-client-listing-right-now-520700a).
- **Reserve with Google:** Platforms must pass Google's technical certification and keep an active booking API connection, and the button lets guests book without leaving Google — [Google Actions Center reservations docs](https://developers.google.com/actions-center/verticals/reservations/e2e/overview); [Servme guide](https://servmeco.com/blog/reserve-with-google-for-restaurants-the-complete-guide/). Partners self-identified in vendor content include TableCheck, Tableo, Quandoo and Servme. UK partners (April 2026) include TheFork, SevenRooms, ResDiary, Mozrest and DesignMyNight — [TableCheck](https://www.tablecheck.com/en/blog/increase-restaurant-reservations-reserve-with-google/); [Tableo](https://tableo.com/reserve-with-google/); [Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/ai/news/booking-restaurants-in-the-uk-just-got-easier-with-ai-in-search-7328d7b7-4a1b-40a6-be9d-b18ed57debde/). Losing the button "could quietly cost your venue thousands" (vendor) — [WowApps](https://www.wowapps.com/reserve-with-google-why-losing-this-button-could-quietly-cost-your-venue-thousands/). Without partner status, restaurants can add a plain reservation link in GBP — [Eat App](https://restaurant.eatapp.co/blog/reservation-link-restaurant-google-business).

### Inferences
- The Pass already pushes hours from service periods, which is the source of truth most listing tools lack (they rely on manual entry or POS). Two gaps to fill. First, a "listing drift" monitor that re-reads GBP regularly, detects Google- or user-applied changes to hours, phone, website and the reserve link, logs them, and reverts or alerts. Second, holiday and special hours generated automatically from closures and events in the reservation system. These address the most common operator complaints directly.
- Hijacking and phone-number swaps directly threaten reservation traffic. Monitoring the phone, website and booking URL fields is high-value and cheap to build.
- Reserve with Google certification is a moat item. Without it, The Pass's booking link can appear only as a plain URL, while OpenTable and SevenRooms-class competitors get the native button.
- Menu sync to Google, Yelp and Apple Maps is Marqii's core differentiator. The Pass would need menu data plus publisher integrations (or a Marqii-style aggregator) to compete there. That is likely lower priority than drift monitoring.

### Gaps
- No official, current US list of Reserve with Google restaurant partners was found, and Resy/Tock/OpenTable participation in 2026 was not confirmed.
- No primary documentation of Apple Maps menu sync or Apple Business Connect API access for third parties was retrieved.
- Owner.com SEO claims were not independently verified.
- Lead-gen scams aimed at restaurants ("Google verification" sales calls) were documented only anecdotally (one forum thread).

## Paid ads: who runs restaurant ads, at what price, and operator complaints

### Takeaway
Restaurants buy ads through general PPC agencies (10–20% of spend, often with a $500+/month minimum or a hybrid base fee), restaurant website platforms selling done-for-you "Boost/paid ads" add-ons (Popmenu), and increasingly marketplace ads (DoorDash Sponsored Listings on cost per order, Uber Eats on cost per click, Yelp on CPC). The persistent complaints are no conversion tracking to real covers, boosted-post waste, agency incentives tied to spend, contract lock-in, and Performance Max opacity. Google improved PMax reporting in 2025 but still does not allow channel-level control.

### Cited Findings
- Agency fees are typically 10–20% of monthly media spend (15% as a default), often with a minimum. Hybrids look like a $1,000 base plus 5%. Critics note the model rewards higher spend regardless of results (mostly agency sources) — [Yael Consulting](https://www.yaelconsulting.com/google-ads-management-fees-unpacking-agency-pricing-models/); [ALM Corp 2026](https://almcorp.com/blog/google-ads-management-pricing-2026/); [ClicksGeek 2026](https://clicksgeek.com/google-ads-account-management-fees/). A restaurant-focused comparison lists PPC management from $500/month and criticizes percentage-of-spend incentives — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/restaurant-marketing-agency).
- Popmenu sells "Boost & paid ads" as an add-on where "industry experts create and execute your marketing strategy," priced on top of $179–$449/mo plans — [Popmenu pricing](https://get.popmenu.com/pricing). No Owner.com paid ads product was found. No "Toast Ads" product for Google/Meta was found (Toast's 2026 agents cover SEO, reviews and marketing) — [NRN](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/toast-unveils-an-array-of-new-ai-agents-for-restaurants).
- **DoorDash Sponsored Listings** use second-price cost-per-acquisition: the restaurant pays only when an order follows an ad click, and the bid caps the cost per order. DoorDash claims $6 in sales per $1 spent and says more than 45% of ad orders come from new customers (company figures). Uber Eats charges per click — [DoorDash help](https://help.doordash.com/en-ca/advertising/article/overview-sponsored-listings); [DoorDash merchants](https://merchants.doordash.com/en-ca/products/sponsored-listings); [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/the-holy-grail-of-e-commerce-advertising-why-doordash-is-bolstering-its-advertising-offerings). An agency executive said marketplace ads put "additional pressure on the restaurants to pay and compete" — [The Daily Upside](https://www.thedailyupside.com/industries/consumer/doordash-launches-ad-platform-accelerating-revenue-race-among-delivery-giants/).
- **Yelp Ads** are CPC with a minimum of about $5/day (about $150/mo). Complaints include unexpected price changes, enhanced profile fees continuing while ads are paused, and early-termination fees. A secondary source reports Yelp FY2025 CPC up 9% while clicks fell 3% (unverified against the 10-K). Guides say restaurants "usually struggle" with Yelp ads — [Media Captain](https://themediacaptain.com/pros-and-cons-of-yelp-advertising); [WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/blog/ppc/yelp-advertising-costs/); [SQ Magazine](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/yelp-statistics/); [Sagapixel](https://sagapixel.com/marketing/yelp-advertising/).
- **Meta waste:** Agencies consistently cite boosted posts to broad audiences, no offer, and no pixel or tracking as the main causes of wasted restaurant Facebook spend. Several "X% wasted" statistics are unsourced (agency content) — [Pixel Bureau](https://pixelbureau.com/blog/business/stop-wasting-your-money-on-facebook-advertising/); [86 Empty Seats](https://86emptyseats.com/blog/restaurant-facebook-ads/); [Digital Restaurateur](https://digitalrestaurateur.com/restaurant-facebook-ads).
- **Attribution:** Restaurant ad profitability depends on tracking reservations, orders and calls as conversions. Last-click misses multi-channel diner journeys, and booked reservations should be reconciled against POS covers and no-shows — [39 Celsius](https://www.39celsius.com/google-ads-for-restaurants/); [Kard](https://www.getkard.com/blog/7-restaurant-marketing-strategies-that-drive-measurable-roi-in-2025); [Avantages Feedback](https://avantagesfeedback.com/en/blog/restaurant-reservations-no-show-service-planning/).
- **Performance Max opacity:** On April 30, 2025 Google announced PMax channel reporting (Search, YouTube, Display, Discover, Gmail, Maps), full search-terms reporting and asset metrics. Channel reporting rolled out from May 30, 2025. Advertisers still cannot shift budget between channels or turn channels off. Google had earlier said PMax "intentionally" doesn't show channel KPIs. Brand-search cannibalization remains a complaint — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-unveils-major-transparency-boost-for-performance-max-campaigns/); [Search Engine Land rollout](https://searchengineland.com/google-begins-rolling-out-performance-max-channel-performance-reporting-456251); [AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/platforms/advertisers-finally-win-key-transparency-concessions-from-googles-performance-max/); [Optmyzr study](https://www.optmyzr.com/blog/performance-max-2025-updates-study-analysis/).
- Agency and vendor contract lock-in shows up in adjacent categories (Podium annual auto-renewal; Yelp termination fees) — see the sources above. No restaurant-ad-agency-specific lock-in data was found.

### Inferences
- The Pass's core advantage is closed-loop attribution: Google/Meta spend measured against seated covers and order revenue by source, not clicks or "reservations started." No competitor found offers this for independents. Agencies report platform metrics, Popmenu/Owner focus on online ordering, and Toast has no paid-ads agent. Push offline conversions (seated covers and their value) back to Google Ads and Meta CAPI so their bidding optimizes for real covers. This fixes the "PMax optimizes for junk conversions" problem and is the biggest concrete gap.
- The pricing story writes itself. An agency at 15% of $3,000/month in spend costs $450/month plus minimums. A flat per-location fee with an agent that proposes changes within hard spending limits undercuts that and removes the agency incentive to raise spend. Show "cost per seated cover" and "cost per new guest," using the loyalty and guest list to identify new versus returning guests, instead of vanity metrics.
- PMax still offers no channel control. The Paid Ads Agent should favor Search plus brand-exclusion strategies for independents, and surface PMax channel or search-term reports as plain-language findings ("38% of PMax spend went to Display/YouTube and produced 0 bookings").
- Marketplace ads (DoorDash/Uber Eats) compete for the same marketing dollars. A unified "where should my next $500 go" view comparing Google/Meta cost per cover with DoorDash cost per order net of commission would be unique. That requires ingesting marketplace ad spend, which was not confirmed to be available by API.
- Brand-name search protection (competitors and OTAs bidding on the restaurant's name) and wasted-spend detection (ads running while the restaurant is fully booked or closed, driven by service periods and availability) are concrete agent policies The Pass can uniquely enforce because it knows capacity.

### Gaps
- Reddit operator threads could not be accessed. Operator complaints are from forums, trade press and agency/vendor content, not first-person Reddit posts.
- No published pricing was found for restaurant-specialist ad agencies (e.g., flat-fee restaurant agencies), Popmenu Boost or ads add-ons (only a competitor's $300 claim for AI marketing), or a Toast ads product.
- No independent data was found on actual DoorDash/Uber Eats cost per order or on merchant ROI.
- Whether DoorDash/Uber Eats expose ad spend to third parties by API is unverified.

## Concrete gaps The Pass can fill (synthesis across all three areas)

### Takeaway
Competitors split into silos: reputation suites (Birdeye/Podium), listings (Marqii/Yext/Moz), POS bundles (Toast, Google only), website/ordering platforms (Owner/Popmenu), and agencies for ads. None ties reviews, listings and ads back to the actual reservation and cover. The Pass's booking data, change log and spend-limited agents address the most-cited complaints: lock-in, wrong hours, canned replies, and ads that cannot be tied to covers.

### Cited Findings
- Toast's reviews tool is Google only, keeps 30 days of history, and has no brand-voice controls yet — [Toast FAQ](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Toast-Reviews-Agent-FAQ).
- Templated, late or bulk replies get silently removed by Google — [SE Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/rejected-google-review-replies-analysis-41457.html).
- Hours get changed by Google, users and robocalls, and suggested edits cannot be disabled — [Synup](https://synup.com/how-to/prevent-suggested-edits-unauthorized-changes-on-your-google-business-profile/); [LocalSearchForum](https://localsearchforum.com/threads/im-getting-calls-from-google-maps-at-650-206-5555-asking-about-clients-hours-are-they-legit.57546/).
- PMax offers no channel-level budget control — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-begins-rolling-out-performance-max-channel-performance-reporting-456251).
- Percentage-of-spend agency fees reward higher spend — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/restaurant-marketing-agency).
- Annual auto-renew contracts and cancellation friction (Podium, Birdeye) — [BBB](https://www.bbb.org/us/ut/lehi/profile/computer-software-developers/podium-1166-90023083/complaints); [CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/review-management/birdeye/hidden-costs/).

### Inferences (prioritized gap list)
1. **Booking-aware review replies:** Join each review to the guest's reservation or order (time, party, server, wait, prior visits). Draft specific replies and flag operational issues ("3 reviews this week mention wait times at Friday 7–8pm, when you were overbooked by 12%"). Cover Yelp and Facebook, not only Google.
2. **Reply hygiene policy in code:** Enforce reply-freshness SLAs (for example, auto-expire or escalate drafts older than 7 days), a duplicate/template similarity check before posting, and owner-defined auto-approve rules for positive reviews. Record everything in change_log.
3. **FTC-safe review requests:** Send post-visit asks to all seated guests regardless of sentiment (no gating), with logs as proof. Private feedback can run in parallel but must not suppress public review links.
4. **Listing drift guard:** Re-read GBP fields on a schedule. Detect Google-, user- or third-party-applied changes to hours, phone, website, reservation URL and categories. Auto-revert where the API allows and alert otherwise. Derive holiday and special hours from reservation-system closures.
5. **Reserve with Google partnership:** This gets the native "Reserve a table" button. It needs Google certification and is a strategic item.
6. **Ads attributed to covers:** Send offline and enhanced conversions (seated covers with value, new versus returning) to Google Ads and Meta CAPI.
